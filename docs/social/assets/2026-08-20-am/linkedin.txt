A story making the rounds today (The Register, 20 Aug 2026): an engineer at Softjourn asked an AI agent to recommend a package for a routine task. The agent came back with a name that looked completely legitimate — familiar format, plausible library.

It wasn't real. Someone had registered that exact name days earlier and loaded it with malware, betting a developer under deadline pressure would install first and check later. Security researchers now have a name for this: slopsquatting — attackers squatting on package names AI models are known to hallucinate.

Softjourn caught it because they have a policy: double-check every AI-recommended package's download count and source before installing. That's the whole story. Not a fancier scanner, not a smarter model — a habit of pausing before the "yes."

That's the same bet I'm building Leash around at ThumbGate: the moment before an agent's suggestion becomes an action is the cheapest place to catch the bad one.
