Saw a story worth sharing here: an engineer asked an AI agent to recommend a package. It looked completely legitimate. It wasn't — someone had registered that exact hallucinated name days earlier and loaded it with malware. Researchers are calling it slopsquatting. (The Register, 20 Aug 2026, via Softjourn's Sergiy Fitsak.)

The only thing that caught it: a team habit of checking download counts and source before installing anything an AI recommends. Not a smarter model. A pause.

I've been building a free approve/deny gate (Leash, part of ThumbGate) around that same instinct — pause the risky action before it runs, not after. No traction numbers to share, this is a solo build-in-public project, but the honest framing seemed worth posting here first.
